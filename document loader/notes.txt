## **LANGUAGE MODELS:**

- A **language model** is a *probability distribution over a sequence of tokens* (words, characters, sub‑words, etc.).
- **Token:** the basic unit (word, character, sub‑word).
    
    **Type:** a *unique* token.
    
    **Vocabulary:** the set of all types extracted from a corpus (collection of words).
    
- Probability formulation:

!Screenshot 2026-04-11 at 4.57.28 PM.png

- “The monsoon rains have arrived” → High probability
    
    “The rains have arrived right on the monsoon” → Low probability 
    
- Syntax vs Semantics in LMs:
    - **Syntactically correct but semantically wrong:**
        - “The cheese eats the man”
    - **Correct sentence:**
        - “The man eats the cheese”
    - LMs capture both:
        - Grammar (syntax)
        - Meaning (semantics)
- An **autoregressive model** generates each token sequentially, conditioning on all previously generated tokens. LMs are autoregressive in nature.

## Large Language Models:

- “Large” refers to the number of parameters and size of training (number of tokens).
- Evolution of Architectures:
    - **1960s:** Rule‑based chatbots (e.g., ELIZA).
    - **1990s–2000s:** RNN, LSTM, GRU to address vanishing gradients.
    - **2017:** Transformer introduced (parallel processing, positional encoding).
    - **Post‑2017:** Shift from RNN‑based to transformer‑based models; rapid scaling of parameters and data.
- AI Hierarchy:
    
    Artificial Intelligence (AI) → Machine Learning (ML) → Deep Learning → Large Language Models (LLMs)
    
- Key Model Families:
    1. Transformers:
        - Two blocks: **Encoder** processes input; **Decoder** generates output.
        - Enables *parallel* processing of tokens, unlike sequential RNNs.
    2. BERT & Encoder-Only Models:
        - Uses only encoder part of the transformer.
        - Trained with **masked language modeling**.
    3. GPT Series(Decoder only):
        - Focuses solely on the **decoder**.
        - Scales from 110 M (GPT‑1) → 175 B (GPT‑3) → >1 T (GPT‑4).
    4. Other Notable Models:
        - **ELMo** – contextual word embeddings.
        - **RoBERTa** – optimised BERT training.
        - **XLM / XLM‑R** – cross‑lingual pre‑training.
        - **Megatron‑LM**, **Gopher**, **OPT** – large open‑source models.
- Zero-shot Learning:
    - No examples given → model still performs task
- Few-shot Learning:
    - Few examples → improves performance
- In-context Learning:
    - Learns from examples **during inference (no training update)**
- Scaling Law:
    - Increasing Model size and Data size leads to better performance and new abilities.
- **Recent Model Highlights:**
    1. **Gemini (Google)**
        - Unified framework consolidating Google’s previous bot models.
        - **Closed‑source**; comparable to OpenAI’s GPT‑4‑style APIs.
    2. **LLaMA 3.1**
        - **>400 B** parameters, largest open‑source Meta model to date.
        - Shows competitive performance with proprietary models on many benchmarks.
    3. **Mistral‑2**
        - Supports **80+ natural languages** and **80+ programming languages**.
        - Benchmarked against LLaMA 3.1 with “reasonable” parity.
    4. **Corstral**
        - Hybrid architecture: transformer‑based language core + **state‑space (Mamba)** sequence modeling.
    5. **Phi‑3 (Microsoft)**
        - Continuation of the tiny‑model line, targeting edge deployment with strong instruction following.

## NLP (Natural Language Processing):

- **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** is the interdisciplinary field of computer science, artificial intelligence, and computational linguistics that focuses on the interaction between computers and human (natural) languages.
- **6,000+** official languages worldwide. Including dialects, the number rises dramatically (e.g., **19,000** languages in India).
- **Alan Turing** – considered the father of NLP.
    
    **Noam Chomsky** – father of modern linguistics; proposed **Universal Grammar.**
    
- Ambiguities in Language:
    - **Lexical & semantic ambiguity** – a single word has multiple meanings (e.g., “Trump” can be a surname or a verb meaning “to outrank”).
    - **Syntactic ambiguity** – the sentence structure lets two parses compete (e.g., “I saw a girl **with a telescope**” – who owns the telescope?).
    - **Pragmatic ambiguity** – the intended meaning depends on context or speaker intent (e.g., “Mujhe aapko mithai khilani padegi” can mean either *I’ll give you sweets* or *you must give me sweets*).
    - **Punctuation ambiguity** – a missing or misplaced comma changes meaning dramatically (“Let’s eat grandma” vs. “Let’s eat, grandma”).
    - **Idiomatic / multi‑word expressions** – the literal words differ from the idiomatic sense (“hot dog” is a food, not a canine that’s warm).
- **NLP Task Categories:**
    1. Natural Language Understanding (NLU)
        - Understanding text
        - Examples:
            - Sentiment analysis
            - POS tagging
            - Parsing
    2. Natural Language Generation (NLG)
        - Generating text
        - Examples:
            - Chatbots
            - Text generation
- **NLP Processing Pipeline:**
    1. **Morphology:**
        - Study of word formation and internal structure (morphemes).
        - **Stemming** – fast algorithm returning a root (may not be a real word).
        - **Lemmatization** – slower but accurate, returns the dictionary form (lemma).
        - English and Chinese are **poor morphologically**; Hindi, Hungarian are **rich** m**orphologically.**
        - Eg - “independently” → in + depend + ent + ly.
    2. **Syntax**:
        - Combining words into grammatical structures.
        - **Part‑of‑Speech (POS) Tagging:**
            - Assigns grammatical categories (noun, verb, etc.) to each token.
            - Example: “park” as noun vs. verb in “He went to the park” vs. “They park the car.”
        - **Chunking:**
            - Grouping adjacent POS‑tagged tokens into higher‑level phrases such as noun phrases (NP) or verb phrases (VP).
            - Example: “He **[NP]** went **[VP]** to **[PP]** Mumbai **[NP]**.”
        - **Parsing:**
            - Builds tree structure of sentence that shows how phrases nest inside one another and how dependencies link words.
    3. **Semantics**:
        - Meaning of words & sentences.
        - Types:
            1. *Decomposition Semantics:* Breaks sentence into entities & attributes (e.g., human, female, adult).
            2. *Ontological Semantics:* How the meaning of the word relates to the meanings of other words. (WordNet is a lexical resource that organizes words according to their semantic relations.)
                
                !Screenshot 2026-04-11 at 7.32.51 PM.png
                
            3. *Distributional Semantics:* Represents words by their surrounding context in large corpora (co‑occurrence matrices) (uses cosine similarity).
    4. **Pragmatics & Discourse:**
        - **Pragmatics** – The study of how context influences the interpretation of utterances, i.e., the negotiation of meaning between speaker and listener.
        - Example: “Can you pass the bottle?” Not a question, it’s a request.
        - **Discourse** - Understanding conversation flow.
- **Tokenization:**
    - Breaking a string of characters into a sequence of words.
- **Tasks we want to solve in NLP:**
    1. **Semantic Role Labelling (SRL):**
        - Assign **semantic roles** (agent, patient, source, destination, etc.) to constituents of a sentence.
        - Example: “John drove Mary from Delhi to Pune in his car.”
            - **Agent**: John
            - **Patient**: Mary
            - **Source**: Delhi
            - **Destination**: Pune
    2. **Textual Entailment:**
        - Determine whether one natural language sentence entails (implies) another under an ordinary interpretation.
        - Example:
            - (Ram hit Shyam with a hockey stick yesterday. → Shyam got hurt) ⇒ Positive TE
            - (Ram hit Shyam with a hockey stick yesterday. → Shyam did not get hurt) ⇒ Negative TE
            - (Ram hit Shyam with a hockey stick yesterday. → Shyam got his first goal) ⇒ Non TE
    3. **Co-reference Resolution:**
        - Identify when different expressions refer to the **same entity** within a discourse.
        - Example: John shows Bob his Toyota yesterday. … that was really nice but **he** likes this one even better.” → Does **he** refer to **John** or **Bob**?
    4. **Information Extraction:**
        - Extraction of relevant piece of information.
        - Named Entity Recognition (NER):
        • Identify names (Proper nouns)
        • [India]Location born [Sundar Pichai]Person is the CEO of [Google]Organization and its parent company [Alphabet]Organization
        - Relation Extraction:
        • Relation among entities
        • CEO(Sundar Pichai, Google)
    5. **Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD):**
        - Choose the correct **sense** of a polysemous word using context.
        - Example: “bank” → *financial institution* vs. *riverbank.*
    6. **Sentiment Analysis:**
        - Classify text as **positive**, **negative**, or **neutral**.
        - Example: It's a mass Chinese product. Too expensive. Thin and useless ⇒ Negative
    7. **Emotion Detection:**
        - Assign one of six emotions (e.g., happy, sad, angry, fear, surprise, disgust).
    8. **Machine Translation:**
        - Given a sentence in the source language L1, convert it to the target language L2, such that the semantic (adequacy and fluency) is preserved.
        - Modern MT still suffers from **gender bias** (e.g., “She is a doctor” → “He is a doctor” after round‑trip translation).
    9. **Summarization:**
        - Given a document, summarize the semantics (extract relevant information) in shorter length text.
    10. **Question Answering (QA) Types:**
        - Answer natural language questions based on information presented in the repository.
        - Fact-based (Who is the author of the book Wings of Fire?)
        - List-based (What are the islands in India?)
        - Descriptive (hardest) (What is Greenhouse effect?)
    11. **Dialogue Systems:**
        - Chatbots (like ChatGPT).
